The approach is sound as an experiment, but **baking bone transforms will not preserve mesh-deform cages, lattices, corrective deformation, or particle hair**. Professional source geometry alone does not guarantee professional exported results.

**Prove the dog first; defer the cat and the full action set.** Author one short, steady walk using the native rig, then test whether a simplified export armature and geometric coat preserve its silhouette and deformation. Compare native evaluated poses against the exported model at contact, mid-stance, and toe-off. Reject the conversion if shoulders, hips, paws, or coat visibly collapse. Do this before investing in sniffing or pivots.

The smallest meaningful walking gate is the exported dog completing several uninterrupted strides along a straight segment **inside the actual Three.js hero**, using the existing simulation’s travel speed. Require:

- Stance paws remain planted in world space, without sliding.
- Stride length and cadence match distance traveled.
- Weight transfer reads clearly, without knee popping or abrupt loop transitions.
- It visibly beats the current walk at the real hero camera and display size.

That validates straight walking only; turns need a separate gate.

Keep the cat on hold until its license permits the intended browser delivery. Private development storage does not resolve eventual asset-extraction restrictions.
